## Start with the handoff

Sales has promised a start date, but the field supervisor still needs the scope. Later, a technician files an update, but the office can’t tell whether the job is ready to invoice. In both cases, someone has to track down a colleague before work can continue.

Before choosing software, I ask the person receiving the job what they need to take it on. We agree on who supplies those details and where an incomplete job goes for review.

## Define the signal before building the report

A pipeline total depends on which opportunities count. A green project status means little if the crew has yet to be assigned or the last field update is a month old.

For each number, I write down where it comes from, when it gets updated, and which decision it should help someone make. If the balance on a job is unknown, the report should show that. A zero tells the person reading it something different.

## Give exceptions somewhere to go

A list of overdue follow-ups should tell a salesperson which customer is waiting, what was promised, and when. I link the reminder to the account so the salesperson can make the call and record the outcome in one place. Once the account is up to date, the reminder should clear.

## Make automation reviewable

Automation works best when the process already has clear boundaries. An account brief can help a salesperson prepare. A schedule preview can show which tasks will move. An estimate can carry its source quantities into review.

Before a tool changes a schedule or issues an invoice, I want to know who authorized it, which records it checked, and what happens if it fails halfway through. Some steps need a person to approve them. Others can run under an agreed rule.

I built those checks into ABS Console. Teams can review a handoff, catch an edit based on an outdated record, and trace an invoice back to the job. [See the workflow in ABS Console.](/abs-tooling#customer-journey)

## Keep the loop short

At a weekly review, I want the team to be able to open a job and answer three questions. What changed? What’s holding it up? Who can move it forward? If someone has to spend Friday afternoon rebuilding that story from emails and spreadsheets, I go back and look at the handoff.
