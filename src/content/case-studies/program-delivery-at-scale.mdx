## From employee one to leading operations

I joined Vale Field Services at startup stage as employee one. In the beginning, I performed the work in the field while developing customer relationships. As the company grew, my role shifted toward the office and the responsibility of organizing the business around a larger team and broader customer commitments.

My remit grew to cover all aspects of the business outside finance. As Head of Strategic Operations, I connected relationship development and customer onboarding with project planning, staffing, dispatch, vendor coordination, field execution, and customer follow-through. Having done the field work myself gave me practical context for the processes and support the teams needed.

## Building the team and operating structure

Vale grew to 35 employees, including four field supervisors, 29 field technicians, and admin/support staff. I managed the four supervisors and built the processes, vendor network, training, and quality standards supporting distributed delivery.

As the organization expanded, the work required a consistent operating structure: clear responsibilities, documented procedures, escalation paths, and a way to carry each job through completion. I owned that structure across onboarding, planning, dispatch, subcontractor management, and field execution.

## Restaurant technology across the U.S. and Canada

We installed point-of-sale (POS) systems and new technology packages for restaurants across the United States and Canada. Our restaurant work included Taco Bell, Dairy Queen, Scooter's, McDonald's, and KFC locations. A typical rollout covered hundreds of stores.

That scale connected customer relationships with the practical demands of organizing distributed teams and vendors. Each program needed a workable deployment plan, an understanding of site readiness, clear direction for the field, and a reliable path through issue resolution and closeout. This restaurant work sat alongside broader technology and facilities programs for retail and commercial customers.

## Before deployment

I served as a strategic pre-deployment partner, identifying feasibility constraints, site-readiness gaps, installation risks, and support requirements. That work informed the execution model and surfaced dependencies before teams arrived on site.

## During delivery

I coordinated delivery through the field supervisors, technicians, and vendor network. SOPs, training materials, performance standards, and escalation paths gave distributed teams a consistent way to work and raise issues.

The program structure connected those individual activities: who was responsible, what needed to be ready, and how an issue moved toward a decision.

## Through closeout

Delivery continued through issue resolution, invoicing readiness, and post-work quality follow-through. Including those steps in the operating model kept the scope broader than the installation date and connected field completion with customer and business requirements.

## Business context

During my July 2019–October 2024 tenure, I helped grow Vale from startup stage to 35 employees and approximately $4M in annual revenue. My contribution evolved from doing the field work and developing relationships to building and running the operations that supported delivery across hundreds of locations.

The processes, vendor networks, training, and quality standards were part of that progression. They gave a growing organization a shared way to plan, execute, and follow through as customer programs expanded across the U.S. and Canada.

That operating experience also informs the [internal systems I build](/technical-projects): clear ownership, visible exceptions, and workflows that carry work through completion.
