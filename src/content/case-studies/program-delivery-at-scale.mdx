## <span id="from-employee-one-to-leading-operations">From installing equipment to running the crews</span>

I was Vale’s first employee. In the beginning I installed the equipment myself, so I got to know our customers in their stores. As we hired more people, I moved into the office to plan the crews’ work. Eventually I ran the day-to-day business apart from finance.

## <span id="building-the-team-and-operating-structure">Four field supervisors and a vendor network</span>

Four field supervisors reported to me. We had 29 technicians in the field and staff in the office. I found the vendors we worked with, wrote our installation procedures, and set the standards for training crews and checking their work.

## Restaurant technology across the U.S. and Canada

We installed point-of-sale systems and other restaurant technology at Taco Bell, Dairy Queen, Scooter’s, McDonald’s, and KFC locations. A typical rollout covered hundreds of stores. We also handled technology and facilities work for retail and commercial customers.

## <span id="before-deployment">Before I sent a crew</span>

I went through the store list with the customer. If a restaurant still had work to do before we could install anything, I sent the crew to one that was ready.

## <span id="during-delivery">While the installers were on site</span>

I worked out who was going where with our supervisors and subcontractors. If an installer got stuck at a store, they knew which supervisor to call.

## <span id="through-closeout">Before the office sent the bill</span>

I checked the installation and followed up with the crew on anything that still needed fixing. Once the work was ready to bill, the office used the crew’s field report to put the invoice together.
