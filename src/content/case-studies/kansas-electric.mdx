## The operating problem
Kansas Electric needed a modern, credible digital presence and internal systems to keep pace with growth. Commercial motion, lead capture, estimating, field reporting, and leadership visibility were spread across disconnected tools, which slowed response times and made pipeline health hard to trust.

As Director of Business Development & Technical Systems from October 2024 through December 2025, I owned both the commercial work and the systems supporting it. The tools covered the path from an incoming request and account research through estimating, field updates, and leadership reporting.

## Constraints
- Small team, limited engineering bandwidth
- Field work with variable schedules and data quality
- Legacy processes and tools spread across email, spreadsheets, and ad-hoc systems
- Needed to deliver improvements without disrupting day-to-day operations

## What I owned
- Rebuilt the public website with clear service positioning and lead capture
- Implemented intake routing so new requests reached the right teams fast
- Built a suite of PWAs and Apps Script tools for estimating, field reporting, and planning
- Connected operational data so pipeline status, capacity, and follow-up were visible
- Documented operating workflows and ownership to keep execution consistent

## How the tools fit together

1. **Intake and account development.** The website and lead capture flow feed a commercial process supported by CRM activity, contact search, and customer research.
2. **Estimating.** Bid packages move into document parsing, structured take-offs, confidence review, and export-ready outputs.
3. **Field execution.** Offline-capable reports collect job updates and media, with structured fields and a summary for review.
4. **Planning and visibility.** Opportunity refreshes, forecasting, and labor rollups give leadership a recurring view of commercial and operational activity.

## Architecture choices

**Make field capture resilient to connectivity.** The PWAs use service worker caching and local IndexedDB storage. Queued field reports can synchronize when a connection becomes available, while the Apps Script service stores media in Drive and report rows in Sheets.

**Use a lightweight operational backbone.** Apps Script and Sheets support scheduled refreshes, standardized schemas, stage-weighted forecasting, and daily labor summaries. That kept smaller tools close to the team's existing workflows. The CRM adds Acumatica integration, charting, and account briefing around the commercial record.

**Keep extraction reviewable.** Estimate Accelerator combines Web Worker parsing, OpenAI Vision, OCR fallback, confidence scoring, and pricing context. Structured take-offs and exports give the estimator a concrete output to review before carrying it into the next step.

**Own the whole delivery path.** The public website uses Astro and Tailwind. Across the internal suite, my work covered the interface, supporting services, GitHub delivery workflows, rollout, and maintenance.

## Commercial results and system outcomes

During my Kansas Electric role, I closed approximately $2M in new work within the first 60 days. The commercial motion was tied to more than $100M in opportunities presented in 2025 and a forward pipeline exceeding $200M over the following 24 months. Presented opportunities and forward pipeline describe commercial activity; they are separate from closed revenue.

The systems supported that work through structured intake, shared account history, reviewable estimating outputs, offline field reporting, and consistent planning rollups. Those are the concrete capabilities delivered alongside the broader business-development work.
