## <span id="from-an-existing-relationship-to-a-walmart-project">How I won the Walmart work</span>

<p id="developing-relationships-across-industrial-and-commercial-markets">Walmart needed emergency lighting and relay work at stores in the Midwest and Southeast. A contact I knew from Vale helped me win the work for Kansas Electric. I stayed on as the person Walmart called and oversaw the project managers doing the work in both regions.</p>

## <span id="building-a-dedicated-sales-function">Why I took over sales</span>

When I joined, project managers were selling the next job while running the current one. I took over the industrial and commercial accounts, working with contractors and business owners. I tracked our conversations and bids, then sat down with leadership to review which jobs we expected to win.

## <span id="taking-ownership-of-marketing">Billboards, the website, and digital advertising</span>

As Director of Marketing, I led the rebrand, rebuilt the website, and designed our signs and billboards. I ran the online ads on a budget of about $5,000 a month and helped with recruiting.

## <span id="constraints">Working around crew schedules and patchy connections</span>

<p id="building-the-systems-behind-the-function">We had a small team and little engineering time. Crews worked changing schedules and sometimes lacked a connection on site. New tools had to fit around active jobs and the spreadsheets people already relied on.</p>

## <span id="architecture-choices">Keeping the apps running</span>

I used a service worker and IndexedDB so crews could open the app and keep a report on the device until they had a connection. Apps Script saved the video in Drive and the report details in Sheets.

<p id="what-i-owned">I built the public website with Astro and Tailwind. I also wrote the instructions for our team and looked after the apps, the services they used, and the GitHub workflows I used to release updates.</p>
