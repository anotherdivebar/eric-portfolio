## From an existing relationship to a Walmart project

**The customer need.** Walmart needed emergency lighting and relay work across locations in the Midwest and Southeast. It was a specific electrical-services opportunity that Kansas Electric could support.

**My approach.** I reached into the network I had built at Vale Field Services and developed the opportunity through an established relationship. I connected the customer's electrical needs to Kansas Electric's services, turning an existing connection into a new business opportunity.

**My ownership.** I owned the customer relationship and stayed involved after the sale, overseeing the project managers responsible for delivering the work across both regions.

**The outcome.** I won the Walmart engagement for Kansas Electric. The story shows the full responsibility I bring to business development: develop the relationship, match a concrete need to the company's capabilities, and remain accountable as the work moves into delivery.

## Building a dedicated sales function

When I joined Kansas Electric, sales responsibilities sat with the project managers. The company wanted to move that work into a dedicated role, but there was no established sales process, program, or strategy to step into. My starting point was to build the function itself.

As Director of Business Development & Technical Systems from October 2024 through December 2025, I developed the sales strategy and the process behind account development, pursuit, and follow-up. I paired relationship building with clearer pipeline reviews, forecasting, activity capture, and leadership reporting. That gave the company a dedicated owner for developing new business alongside the teams responsible for delivering it.

## Taking ownership of marketing

The role quickly expanded to include the Director of Marketing title and responsibilities. I led the rebrand and created the company's digital and offline marketing materials, including new signage and billboard designs. I also owned digital marketing campaigns with approximately $5,000 per month in ad spend.

The website rebuild, service positioning, and lead capture flow were part of that broader responsibility. My work connected how the company presented itself with how it found opportunities and followed through on them. I also supported hiring and recruiting as part of the wider business effort.

## Building the systems behind the function

Commercial activity, lead capture, estimating, field reporting, and leadership visibility were spread across email, spreadsheets, and disconnected tools. Alongside the sales and marketing work, I built applications covering the path from an incoming request and account research through estimating, field updates, and leadership reporting.

## Constraints
- Small team, limited engineering bandwidth
- Field work with variable schedules and data quality
- Legacy processes and tools spread across email, spreadsheets, and ad-hoc systems
- Needed to deliver improvements without disrupting day-to-day operations

## What I owned
- Developed the sales strategy, process, and program to establish dedicated commercial ownership
- Led the rebrand, digital and offline marketing materials, signage, billboards, and paid campaigns
- Owned customer relationships and oversaw project managers on the Walmart engagement
- Rebuilt the public website with clear service positioning and lead capture
- Implemented intake routing so new requests reached the right teams fast
- Built a suite of PWAs and Apps Script tools for estimating, field reporting, and planning
- Connected operational data so pipeline status, capacity, and follow-up were visible
- Documented operating workflows and ownership to keep execution consistent

## How the tools fit together

1. **Intake and account development.** The website and lead capture flow feed a commercial process supported by CRM activity, contact search, and customer research.
2. **Estimating.** Bid packages move into document parsing, structured take-offs, confidence review, and export-ready outputs.
3. **Field execution.** Offline-capable reports collect job updates and media, with structured fields and a summary for review.
4. **Planning and visibility.** Opportunity refreshes, forecasting, and labor rollups give leadership a recurring view of commercial and operational activity.

## Architecture choices

**Make field capture resilient to connectivity.** The PWAs use service worker caching and local IndexedDB storage. Queued field reports can synchronize when a connection becomes available, while the Apps Script service stores media in Drive and report rows in Sheets.

**Use a lightweight operational backbone.** Apps Script and Sheets support scheduled refreshes, standardized schemas, stage-weighted forecasting, and daily labor summaries. That kept smaller tools close to the team's existing workflows. The CRM adds Acumatica integration, charting, and account briefing around the commercial record.

**Keep extraction reviewable.** Estimate Accelerator combines Web Worker parsing, OpenAI Vision, OCR fallback, confidence scoring, and pricing context. Structured take-offs and exports give the estimator a concrete output to review before carrying it into the next step.

**Own the whole delivery path.** The public website uses Astro and Tailwind. Across the internal suite, my work covered the interface, supporting services, GitHub delivery workflows, rollout, and maintenance.

## Commercial results and system outcomes

During my Kansas Electric role, I closed approximately $2M in new work within the first 60 days. The commercial motion was tied to more than $100M in opportunities presented in 2025 and a forward pipeline exceeding $200M over the following 24 months. Presented opportunities and forward pipeline describe commercial activity; they are separate from closed revenue.

The broader contribution was building a sales and marketing function where there had previously been no dedicated sales role: strategy, account development, brand ownership, campaign execution, and follow-through into delivery. The internal systems supported that function through structured intake, shared account history, faster estimating, offline field reporting, and consistent planning rollups.
