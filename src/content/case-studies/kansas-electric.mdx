## Developing relationships across industrial and commercial markets

I worked with contractors and business owners to understand their electrical needs, find work Kansas Electric could deliver, and stay in touch as plans developed.

## From an existing relationship to a Walmart project

Walmart needed emergency lighting and relay work at locations in the Midwest and Southeast. A relationship I’d built at Vale Field Services helped me win the work for Kansas Electric. After the sale I remained the customer’s point of contact and oversaw the project managers responsible for both regions.

## Building a dedicated sales function

When I arrived, project managers handled sales alongside the jobs they were delivering. I took responsibility for finding and developing accounts. I set up a sales process, recorded customer conversations, and reviewed the pipeline and forecast with leadership.

## Taking ownership of marketing

I also became Director of Marketing. I led the rebrand, rebuilt the website, designed signage and billboards, and ran digital campaigns with about $5,000 in monthly ad spend. I also helped with recruiting.

## Building the systems behind the function

Sales activity lived in inboxes and spreadsheets. Estimators pulled information from bid documents by hand, and field updates arrived in different formats. I built tools for those jobs while running sales and marketing.

## Constraints

We had a small team and little engineering time. Crews worked changing schedules and sometimes lacked a connection on site. New tools had to fit around active jobs and the spreadsheets people already relied on.

## What I owned

I ran sales and marketing, handled account follow-up, and kept the Walmart engagement on track after the sale. For the internal apps, I wrote the interface and services, documented the procedures, and supported rollout and maintenance.

## How the tools fit together

1. **Accounts.** Requests from the website went to the right team. The CRM kept contact details, account research, and conversations together.
2. **Estimates.** Estimators uploaded bid documents, checked the extracted scope and quantities, and exported a take-off.
3. **Field reports.** Crews recorded updates and media offline, then reviewed a summary before submitting the report.
4. **Planning.** Managers used opportunity updates, forecasts, and daily labor summaries to plan sales follow-up and staffing.

## Architecture choices

**Offline field reports.** I used service worker caching and IndexedDB to keep reports on the device until the crew had a connection. Apps Script stored the media in Drive and the report rows in Sheets.

**Planning in Sheets.** The teams already worked in spreadsheets. Apps Script let me schedule updates, standardize fields, weight forecasts by sales stage, and send daily labor summaries. The CRM also synced with Acumatica.

**Estimator review.** Estimate Accelerator uses a Web Worker to parse documents, with OpenAI Vision and OCR as a fallback. Estimators see confidence scores and historical prices before they export a take-off.

**Rollout and maintenance.** I built the public site with Astro and Tailwind and maintained the internal apps, their supporting services, and the GitHub workflows used to ship changes.

## Commercial results and system outcomes

I closed about $2M in new work in my first 60 days. The other figures describe prospective work. Opportunities presented in 2025 exceeded $100M, and the pipeline for the following 24 months exceeded $200M.

The estimating app reduced the time needed to prepare a bid by about 50–60%. Estimating accuracy improved by about 25%.
