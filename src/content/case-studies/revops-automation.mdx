## Where this approach applies

This brief describes my approach to lifecycle automation. It applies when teams spend too much time reconciling records, ownership is unclear at handoff, or a risk signal arrives without a practical next step.

The useful unit of automation is a complete workflow: a dependable input, an understandable rule, an accountable owner, and a way to see whether the action happened.

## Start with the input

Validation and enrichment should make records more usable before they reach the next team. Missing or inconsistent information needs to be visible so the workflow can direct it to review instead of silently carrying the problem forward.

Shared field and lifecycle definitions are part of that work. A routing rule or risk alert is only as useful as the information it depends on.

## Connect the signal to an action

A pipeline or renewal signal needs context: what changed, who owns the follow-up, and what response is expected. SLA monitoring and escalation make those expectations visible when the normal workflow stalls.

I keep the action path close to the signal so teams can move from reviewing a record to deciding what to do about it.

## Review the exceptions

Exception queues and recurring reviews help distinguish a data problem, an unclear policy, and a missed handoff. That feedback can improve the underlying workflow as teams adopt it.
