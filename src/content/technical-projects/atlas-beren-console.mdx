## The problem

A business can have a CRM, a project board, accounting software, and several automations and still lose the thread between them. Research stays in a browser tab. A salesperson's promise never reaches the delivery plan. A finished job has costs attached but no reviewed invoice. Leadership sees separate reports instead of the work that needs a decision.

I built ABS Tooling around those transitions. Atlas-Beren Console started as an operator workspace for client context, agents, approvals, and prospecting. It has grown into a modular platform that connects acquisition, delivery, financial planning, and service operations.

My work covers the product model, interface, business rules, integrations, database access policies, and verification. The recurring design question is practical: **what does the operator need to know, review, and do next?**

## Setup and daily work

### Start with the customer’s own records

Customer onboarding connects verified sign-in, business setup, reviewed imports, and the first useful action. A resumable guide reads the workspace’s actual records to show what is complete. It does not treat clicking through instructions as proof that setup worked.

CSV and Excel imports provide worksheet selection, column mapping, editable previews, and explicit review before saving. Original files and ignored columns stay out of the saved preview. Matching uses identifiers such as source IDs, domains, and contact email; ambiguous conflicts are held for review, and blank imported cells do not erase existing information. Imported jobs remain unpriced until someone reviews an estimate.

Customer identity also carries into the sales workflow through an explicit link. An existing customer should not become a cold outreach target because two parts of the system disagree about who they are.

### Put the next useful action first

The **Today** view prioritizes urgent and overdue work, then offers contextual actions for follow-ups, projects, marketing, financial findings, and jobs. Operators can work from the record that needs attention. Viewers receive review-only controls, and unavailable sources produce an incomplete-coverage notice rather than an all-clear message.

Guided estimates move through customer, work and price, then review and save. Guided projects turn a goal, owner, and date into persisted tasks; progress comes from those saved task statuses. Drafts retain interrupted inputs within their user and workspace scope. Stale records and uncertain saves are surfaced before a retry can duplicate work. Supported task edits also offer revision-aware undo.

Across the seven modules, performance views connect summaries and charts back to actionable records. They show the date basis and source coverage behind a number: estimated pipeline remains distinct from sales, and missing data is not displayed as zero.

## Sales and research

### From company discovery to a defensible next step

Prospect Finder uses Overture Maps place data, selecting relevant geographic data and row groups instead of loading an entire dataset. Company research checks a bounded set of public website pages, surfaces identity conflicts, and retains the sources behind an account assessment.

The review workspace combines fit scoring, website observations, enrichment, and shortlist decisions. Operators can save a named review queue, resume unfinished research, and skip accounts already reviewed. A website audit can become a shareable report rather than disappearing into a chat response.

I built researched campaigns around named recipients, company and role evidence, and fresh email verification. ## Projects and delivery

### Carry the sales context into the delivery plan

A won deal can become a linked project through an explicit review of scope, owner, dates, and billing expectations. The server checks that the source deal belongs to the workspace and is still valid. Repeated handoffs reopen the existing linked project instead of creating duplicates.

Guided playbooks expand into real project tasks with owners, dates, dependencies, and review instructions. On-demand recipes preview changes such as assigning unowned work or escalating overdue tasks before applying them. Guided project progress now follows saved task completion, while shared approvals retain their own review requirements.

### Make schedule and capacity decisions visible

Dependency-aware rescheduling previews the selected task and affected successors, preserves durations, and rejects conflicts or cycles. Approved changes save together with before-and-after history.

Portfolio capacity compares estimated effort with weekly availability. Saved plans can account for holidays and owner time off, making overload visible before work is reassigned. These are planning estimates, not timekeeping or automatic resource scheduling.

## Finance and operations

### Cash planning with the assumptions attached

The finance workspace combines cash, receivables, aging, and scenario planning. Operators can save named scenarios, compare collection-delay assumptions, add recurring adjustments, and inspect a 13-week projection against a minimum reserve.

Financial summaries distinguish missing data from zero, keep currencies separate, and disclose freshness and source coverage. Native issued invoices feed receivables and cash planning net of recorded receipts and reversals. An imported mirror is suppressed only when it explicitly identifies the same native invoice in the same workspace; the system does not guess from similar names or amounts. Recording a receipt against an ABS invoice does not pretend to update a bank account.

### Estimate, execute, maintain, and bill

Connected Operations combines **eight configurable workflows** around shared customers, estimates, jobs, assets, inventory, and costs. Document takeoff supports reviewed PDF, image, and text evidence; calibrated counts, lengths, and areas; and itemized estimating with waste and markup calculations.

AI extraction is limited to stated quantities with page or excerpt evidence. Missing scope stays a gap for a person to resolve. Original documents are archived privately with content hashes, incomplete estimates can be reopened, and accepted revisions retain the earlier versions.

Accepted estimates carry itemized expectations into linked jobs. Completion records actual material, labor, equipment, and subcontract costs. Stock-linked materials retain their cost basis and deduct quantities once. Maintenance workflows can create the next preventive visit from completed service work.

### Customer documents and reviewed delivery

Saved estimates and invoices can produce native PDFs with itemized amounts, pagination, and customer-facing terms. Internal costs stay out of the document. The delivery screen lets an owner or administrator review the recipient, subject, message, and exact PDF before sending; invoice delivery requires an issued record.

Delivery history retains the document snapshot, digest, recipient, and provider outcome. If a response is uncertain, automatic retries stop. A “sent” result means the provider accepted the message, not that the customer read it. This extends the product from internal billing records to a deliberate customer communication workflow.

## Workforce and business operations

I expanded the existing Operations module with scheduling, purchasing, inventory, production, procedures, labor, and an action inbox. Shared people and location records support service businesses, light manufacturing, and restaurant operations through different terminology and presets. These are operating areas within the module, not seven additional platform entitlements.

### Schedule people and resources

Managers build draft schedules, review conflicts, publish shifts, and reuse weekly templates. Checks cover overlapping assignments, time off, required skills, certification expiry, overnight work, and time-zone transitions. An authenticated employee view limits access to the person’s own published shifts, requests, and time records; managers review coverage changes and approved hours can be exported.

### Carry purchasing into stock and production

Purchasing moves from a request through approval, a manually recorded order, and partial receipts. Receiving updates stock once. The lot ledger retains append-only movements, reservations, expiry, and reorder context by item and location.

Production snapshots the bill of materials, reserves eligible stock by earliest expiry, records consumption, and follows routing steps through quality review. Accepted output becomes a finished lot. Rejected output preserves the material-consumption history instead of inventing finished stock.

## A connected workflow

This is how the implemented handoffs support a service job. Each step preserves the context needed by the next, while keeping consequential decisions visible.

<ol class="workflow-steps">
  <li><strong>Review the scope and evidence</strong><p>Inspect the source document, quantities, missing information, and itemized estimate. Save a draft until the scope is ready.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Accept the estimate</strong><p>Carry the agreed scope and expected costs into a linked delivery job, retaining the accepted estimate version.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Complete the work</strong><p>Record delivery details and itemized actuals. Apply stock movements once and preserve job history.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Review and issue the invoice</strong><p>Use the completed job and agreed price, confirm billing and tax details, then freeze the issued record.</p></li>
  <li><strong>Track the balance and the next visit</strong><p>Record received payments against the invoice. Where maintenance applies, keep the next service visit connected to the asset.</p></li>
</ol>

## Engineering decisions

### Shared business rules, separate execution contexts

The application uses Next.js, React, and TypeScript, with Supabase authentication, Postgres, row-level security, and private file storage. Vercel supports deployment and scheduled work; AI Gateway and provider-specific adapters support configured live workflows.

Demo and authenticated surfaces reuse business rules while keeping state isolated. Demonstrating an estimate, scenario, or project does not require customer records or invoke a live delivery adapter. Production failures do not silently fall back to demo persistence.

### Permissions follow the workspace

A common module inventory drives navigation and server enforcement. Customer organizations have their own memberships and enabled modules; agency access is explicitly delegated. Role and tenant checks happen at the API and database layers. Hiding a button is only the interface expression of an enforced permission.

### Protect the handoffs, not just the happy path

Operational edits use revision checks and retained history. Stale previews are rejected before a recipe or schedule update is applied. Database uniqueness protects deal-to-project handoffs from duplication.

Billing uses separate normalized tables for invoices, immutable payment entries, invoice events, and idempotent commands. Atomic database operations and locks protect repeated requests and concurrent changes. The design preserves what happened, even when an operator needs to correct it.

### Give AI a bounded job

Agents carry evidence requirements, readiness checks, structured outputs, and review paths. The live runner validates its output before persistence; a proposed action remains distinct from permission to execute it.

I also built a local MCP interface with **ten scoped tools**: six reads and four controlled writes for drafts, tasks, and prospect review. The tools use shared services, validated inputs, sanitized summaries, and audit logging. They expose specific business actions rather than arbitrary database or shell access.

## Delivery and boundaries

This case study reflects a review of the application source, release notes, tests, and demonstration artifacts on **September 30, 2026**. The platform now connects customer setup, research and outreach, daily tasks, project delivery, cash planning, customer documents, and broader business operations.

The September 24 document-delivery release records **1,246 passing tests and one skip**, alongside TypeScript, production build, database policy checks, and PDF pagination review. Email delivery was simulated during verification. These are documented release checks, not a claim that this portfolio review reran the entire upstream suite.

The September 30 daily-work report records 66 focused tests passing, with type, build, and lint checks passing. Its broader run recorded 1,335 passes, one skip, and two unresolved authentication-fallback test failures during concurrent changes. That is useful evidence of the work’s scope, but not a clean release certificate for every current change.

### Current local development

The latest uncommitted work extends the commercial journey with recorded customer acceptance, assigned field work, service records, completion snapshots, and more detailed invoice scope. It also adds inventory ownership controls, durable operational records, and usage-budget foundations. These changes have local implementations and verification artifacts; production rollout is a separate milestone.

The remaining boundaries are concrete: customer acceptance is not an electronic-signature service, recorded payments are not payment processing, labor export is not payroll, and local drafts are not cross-device offline synchronization. Financial projections remain assumption-driven, and larger deployments must respect the current bounded storage models.

The companion [Atlas-Beren website project](/technical-projects/atlas-beren-website) explains how I made these workflows understandable through public product pages, fictional demonstrations, and contextual inquiries.

What this build demonstrates is the connection between business process design and implementation: ownership, permissions, evidence, review, persistence, and recovery. A new capability has to support the next person’s work as well as the current screen.
