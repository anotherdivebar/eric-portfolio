## The problem

A business can have a CRM, a project board, accounting software, and several automations and still lose the thread between them. Research stays in a browser tab. A salesperson's promise never reaches the delivery plan. A finished job has costs attached but no reviewed invoice. Leadership sees separate reports instead of the work that needs a decision.

I built ABS Tooling around those transitions. Atlas-Beren Console started as an operator workspace for client context, agents, approvals, and prospecting. It has grown into a modular platform that connects acquisition, delivery, financial planning, and service operations.

My work covers the product model, interface, business rules, integrations, database access policies, and verification. The recurring design question is practical: **what does the operator need to know, review, and do next?**

## Setup and daily work

### Start with the customer’s own records

Customer onboarding connects verified sign-in, business setup, reviewed imports, and the first useful action. A resumable guide reads the workspace’s actual records to show what is complete. It does not treat clicking through instructions as proof that setup worked.

CSV and Excel imports provide worksheet selection, column mapping, editable previews, and explicit review before saving. Original files and ignored columns stay out of the saved preview. Matching uses identifiers such as source IDs, domains, and contact email; ambiguous conflicts are held for review, and blank imported cells do not erase existing information. Imported jobs remain unpriced until someone reviews an estimate.

Customer identity also carries into the sales workflow through an explicit link. An existing customer should not become a cold outreach target because two parts of the system disagree about who they are.

### Put the next useful action first

The **Today** view prioritizes urgent and overdue work, then offers contextual actions for follow-ups, projects, marketing, financial findings, and jobs. Operators can work from the record that needs attention. Sales follow-ups now carry the account name, location, pipeline stage, and scheduled follow-up time through Today, the inbox, and the task panel. Viewers receive review-only controls, and unavailable sources produce an incomplete-coverage notice rather than an all-clear message.

Guided estimates move through customer, work and price, then review and save. Guided projects turn a goal, owner, and date into persisted tasks; progress comes from those saved task statuses. Drafts retain interrupted inputs within their user and workspace scope. Stale records and uncertain saves are surfaced before a retry can duplicate work. Supported task edits also offer revision-aware undo.

Across the seven modules, performance views connect summaries and charts back to actionable records. They show the date basis and source coverage behind a number: estimated pipeline remains distinct from sales, and missing data is not displayed as zero.

## Connected records and handoffs

### Carry established context into the next step

I connected estimates, CRM opportunities, Marketing sites and work items, and Financial entries to shared customers and projects. Customer profiles and project workspaces expose those original records through searchable, paged lists. A connection changes where the record can be found; it does not copy a balance or create another transaction.

Known relationships now follow the workflow automatically. Starting an estimate from a project fills its customer and saves the project link with the estimate. Revisions retain that connection, and acceptance attaches the job to the same project. A won opportunity reuses its connected delivery project. Reconciled accounting copies inherit the native invoice's job, customer, and project context.

Marketing work follows its site's connections unless an operator records an exception. Financial imports retain established links when refreshed. Customer identity is determined by explicit records, never a matching name or the assumption that a customer has only one project.

### Ask only when a decision is missing

Complete relationships stay quiet. Missing or conflicting context opens a focused review after the current editor closes; internal work can explicitly remain unconnected. Choosing a project can fill an empty customer selection, while conflicting choices require review. Optional follow-up tasks remain separate from linking the source.

Saved relationships respect module permissions and workspace boundaries. Stale edits and changed parent relationships trigger review; hidden links cannot be silently removed. Source saves and their required connections succeed together, preventing a partially connected customer or estimate.

## Sales and research

### From company discovery to a defensible next step

Prospect Finder uses Overture Maps place data, selecting relevant geographic data and row groups instead of loading an entire dataset. Company research checks a bounded set of public website pages, surfaces identity conflicts, and retains the sources behind an account assessment.

The review workspace combines fit scoring, website observations, enrichment, and shortlist decisions. Operators can save a named review queue, resume unfinished research, and skip accounts already reviewed. A website audit can become a shareable report rather than disappearing into a chat response.

Missing listing websites now trigger a bounded public search. Candidate sites must agree with company identity evidence before automatic matching; ambiguous results require review. Operators can confirm a URL themselves, and a failed search never becomes proof that a website does not exist. Confirmed addresses survive incomplete future discovery rows. Changing the domain clears incompatible research so evidence from one company site cannot silently follow another.

CRM settings support additional open stages within seven reporting categories, with ordering, probabilities, required fields, custom fields, and assignment rules. Duplicate review links canonical accounts while preserving their source history. Forecast snapshots, conversion, aging, and loss reasons make pipeline movement inspectable. Saved personal or team searches can compare changes on demand; passed prospects leave active results but remain available for review and restoration.

### Outreach with an execution model

I built researched campaigns around named recipients, company and role evidence, and fresh email verification. Operators can edit sequences, review messages, set schedules and volume caps, and use configured Zoho Mail delivery and reply synchronization. Reply triage and confirmed outcomes connect the response back to the sales workflow.

Execution supports individual approvals or explicitly authorized campaign rules. Send claims and content fingerprints protect dispatch from duplicate work. If a provider response leaves delivery uncertain, the message becomes unknown and automatic retries stop—sending twice is not an acceptable way to resolve uncertainty.

The newer drafting integration uses OpenAI to turn researched account context and the selected offer into structured email drafts. Server-side validation rejects incomplete or malformed responses before they enter the workflow. Draft generation remains separate from approval and delivery; a generated message still needs review for business identity, factual fit, and clear customer language. Campaign copy revisions preserve sent-message history and require renewed approval of changed drafts.

### Connect the mailbox without turning every sender into a lead

The Zoho integration now reads Inbox and Sent, including messages sent directly in the mailbox. Exact contact matches add subjects and plain-text previews to CRM history. Unknown correspondents with potential business relevance enter a separate review queue; an administrator must approve a candidate before a new lead is created. Existing-company evidence, ambiguous identities, exclusions, and suppressed addresses prevent an automatic duplicate account.

Prospect Finder also supports a reviewed first-contact message. The operator checks current research, a verified recipient, the exact email, and a follow-up date before authorizing Zoho delivery. Accepted sends record the activity and reminder; a later Sent-folder import recognizes the same message. This is a single introduction, with no automatic follow-up sequence started by that approval.

Growth reporting groups recorded first sends into conversion cohorts and separates fully observed windows from immature ones. CRM forecasts can show sensitivity to delivery gaps using an explicit risk assumption. Both expose their coverage and assumptions; neither turns an observed association into a causal result or a guaranteed sale.

### Marketing as delivery work

The marketing module brings website audits, content drafts, editorial calendars, campaign planning, and review stages into a client-aware workspace. A board shows work moving toward review and completion; a calendar puts publishing targets and unscheduled work in context. Stage changes use revision checks so an older edit cannot silently overwrite a teammate's work.

Campaign economics now connects reviewed expenses and call, web, or confirmed-email evidence to booked and completed jobs. A single current campaign assignment per job prevents duplicate credit. Reports expose cost per booking, collections relative to spend, and invoiced contribution after recorded job costs and campaign expenses. Reversals retain the original expense history. Incomplete costs make contribution provisional; reviewed attribution does not establish that a campaign caused the sale, and overhead remains outside the calculation.

Established campaign attribution now follows the opportunity through estimating, delivery, issued invoices, and payments. Recurring visits preserve that lineage in their own service periods. Ambiguous or competing campaign sources remain unassigned instead of being credited by a name or amount match.

## Customer communications

### Keep the conversation beside the work

The shared customer inbox brings email and SMS into a queue with customer and job links, ownership, unread status, and delivery attention filters. Operators can compose a reply, review its destination and purpose, and cancel a queued message. Service consent and marketing consent remain separate. Provider opt-outs block pending sends, and a new customer link requires fresh consent review.

Zoho mailbox imports retain provider message identities and deduplicate repeated reads. Twilio callbacks verify the provider signature and the expected account, sender, destination, and message. Delivery history distinguishes a queued message, provider acceptance, confirmed delivery, and an uncertain result. An uncertain send is held for review instead of being automatically sent again.

### Send updates from current job evidence

Reviewed rules prepare dispatch reminders, en-route and delay updates, completion notices, and quote follow-ups. A preview shows the exact copy, recipients, and blocked conditions. Rules begin paused; the queue checks current consent, sending hours, rule revisions, and the underlying job or estimate before dispatch.

Rescheduling invalidates old reminders, a newer travel update replaces an older one, and quote acceptance cancels an obsolete follow-up. Technicians can record travel updates for their assigned work. This ties customer communication to current operational evidence without requiring someone to copy the same update into another tool.

### Connect Gmail, Outlook, and primary calendars

Workspace administrators can connect a shared Gmail or Microsoft 365 account, then review team visibility before enabling synchronization. Incoming and sent history, authenticated attachment downloads, mailbox selection for replies, and a default sender for reminders join the existing inbox. Exact unique customer email matches link automatically; ambiguous senders require review.

Primary calendars bring upcoming events beside the work. A reviewed job link can carry later schedule changes to an unchanged provider event without invitees. External edits and cancellations are held for review, preserving dispatch constraints and local history. Publishing a calendar entry does not email invitations.

These adapters were verified with simulated provider responses. Live OAuth consent, mailbox/calendar operation, and SMS delivery still require configured accounts and acceptance testing. The initial worker rotates one mailbox per five-minute run; this is a bounded implementation, with higher-volume processing still to be developed.

## Customer portal

Customers can now review a shared quote, approve its exact saved scope and terms, request service, inspect confirmed appointments, and view customer-facing service history, invoices, credits, receipts, and remaining balances. Payment access uses the same balance and checkout controls as the office workflow.

An invitation is tied to the intended verified email. Claiming it grants customer access without making the recipient a workspace member. Access can be revoked; internal notes, private costs, and office-only historical originals stay outside the portal. Changed quotes require sharing again, while earlier accepted snapshots remain in history.

Office review turns a service request into an unpriced job with the customer's details carried forward. Requested dates are preferences: the portal does not yet reserve an available slot or let customers reschedule confirmed work themselves.

## Projects and delivery

### Carry the sales context into the delivery plan

A won deal can become a linked project through an explicit review of scope, owner, dates, and billing expectations. The server checks that the source deal belongs to the workspace and is still valid. Repeated handoffs reopen the existing linked project instead of creating duplicates.

Guided playbooks expand into real project tasks with owners, dates, dependencies, and review instructions. On-demand recipes preview changes such as assigning unowned work or escalating overdue tasks before applying them. Guided project progress now follows saved task completion, while shared approvals retain their own review requirements.

### Make schedule and capacity decisions visible

Dependency-aware rescheduling previews the selected task and affected successors, preserves durations, and rejects conflicts or cycles. Approved changes save together with before-and-after history.

Portfolio capacity compares estimated effort with weekly availability. Saved plans can account for holidays and owner time off, making overload visible before work is reassigned. Capacity remains a planning estimate. Separate approved time records now support task-hour comparisons and reviewed job or project labor allocation, with checks against counting the same person’s time twice.

### Keep collaboration and contribution with the project

Projects now include member assignments, comments and mentions, private files, notifications, evidence-backed approvals, recurring task generation, schedule baselines, and dependencies across projects. A permission-aware customer history connects related sales, jobs, assets, estimates, projects, billing, and labor without copying the authoritative records.

Project planning keeps accepted scope and forecasts visible. The newer shared profit report uses issued net invoice revenue and an explicit labor basis, with the same sources carried into customer and campaign views. Coverage warnings make unresolved allocations and missing costs explicit; this is an operational contribution view rather than a complete profit-and-loss statement.

### Forecast the cost of finishing the work

Cost-to-complete reviews combine recorded costs with itemized remaining labor, materials, subcontracting, equipment, and other costs. The operator selects one labor basis, sets a reviewed budget, and explains the assumptions. Open purchase commitments set a minimum for remaining costs instead of being added a second time.

Each saved version retains its source figures, assumptions, forecast contribution, budget variance, reviewer, and timestamp. A changed cost source or a changed link to a delivery job requires a fresh review—even when the totals happen to match. This makes movement between forecasts explainable and gives the later accounting workpaper a specific reviewed starting point.

## Connected profit and costs

Financial, Projects, Customers, and Marketing now use the same source snapshot and profit calculator. Issued invoice subtotals contribute revenue once. Tax, receipts, deposits, and explicitly linked accounting copies do not add revenue again. Credits reduce revenue; refunds reduce collections without a second revenue reduction. Reports separate currencies and expose their included and excluded sources.

### Carry material and labor evidence into the result

Purchasing links received stock to existing supplier bills and bank-payment records. Receiving inventory does not immediately expense it to a project. Job consumption carries the lot's recorded cost into reporting; transfers preserve it, and unused-stock returns credit the original consumption cost. Matched inventory bills and settlements are excluded from a second cost entry.

Published shifts prefill worked-time records with the person, location, job or project, and scheduled interval. Staff review actual time before approval. Connected approved time creates one labor allocation using the employee's effective rate, burden, and overhead for the work date. Later rate changes do not rewrite history, and scheduled hours alone create no cost. Missing links or rates require a focused review.

Approved labor replaces completion labor according to the report's selected basis. Payroll transfers and imported settlements do not add that cost again. Loaded project cost supports profitability; it does not calculate net pay or statutory overtime.

### Make incomplete or duplicate costs visible

Imported bills and transactions retain a reviewed classification: additional cost or revenue, an already included source, or an exclusion. Changed amounts, tax evidence, or recognition status invalidate the review. Unresolved entries, incomplete job costs, and ambiguous attribution make coverage provisional. The result is invoiced revenue less recorded costs, with any unrecorded overhead still outside the calculation.

## Business simulator

### Ask whether the next job fits the business

I built a deterministic simulator that compares up to eight proposed jobs against existing commitments over a **13-week planning window**. Operators can import draft estimates or enter assumptions, choose which jobs to include, and review the effect on daily cash, qualified labor, contribution, and material availability before committing to the work.

The model gives existing commitments priority. Labor calculations account for skills, locations, availability, approved leave, certification expiry, and employee hour limits. Proposed jobs compete for available stock in start-date order, with checks for expiry, incoming supply, and materials already committed elsewhere. Missing information stays visible as unknown.

Cash calculations distinguish incremental job spending from payroll already included in baseline outflows. Reserve warnings use the lowest daily balance, so a positive weekly closing balance cannot hide an earlier modeled shortfall. Additional crews, overtime, or subcontractors carry explicit dates, hours, skills, and costs.

### Compare alternatives and retain the prediction

The simulator tests alternatives through the same model: a higher deposit, a later start, or additional qualified capacity. Each option shows recalculated cash, contribution, staffing costs, and unresolved constraints. Operators can apply the assumptions and run the model again; the suggestions do not automatically accept an estimate, reserve a crew, or buy stock.

Reusable plans store editable assumptions. A separate frozen prediction preserves the source context, model version, inputs, warnings, and calculated result. The server recomputes that result when saving. Append-only observations with evidence references then compare recorded hours, revenue, contribution, and final payment timing against the prediction.

This is decision support built from explicit assumptions. Daily labor allocation does not establish a feasible timed route, and the cash model does not infer taxes, debt, or unrecorded obligations. The saved history makes the forecast inspectable without presenting it as a guaranteed outcome.

## Finance and operations

### Cash planning with the assumptions attached

The finance workspace combines cash, receivables, aging, and scenario planning. Operators can save named scenarios, compare collection-delay assumptions, add recurring adjustments, and inspect a 13-week projection against a minimum reserve.

Financial summaries distinguish missing data from zero, keep currencies separate, and disclose freshness and source coverage. Native issued invoices feed receivables and cash planning net of recorded receipts and reversals. An imported mirror is suppressed only when it explicitly identifies the same native invoice in the same workspace; the system does not guess from similar names or amounts. Recording a receipt against an ABS invoice does not pretend to update a bank account. Optional Xero and QuickBooks connectors add reviewed company selection, encrypted credentials, explicit enable/pause controls, and atomic invoice and bill imports. Failed or incomplete imports preserve prior balances and expose connection health. Reviewed journal posting is a separate approval flow described below; bank feeds and bank reconciliation remain outside this integration.

### Customer documents and reviewed delivery

Saved estimates and invoices can produce native PDFs with itemized amounts, pagination, and customer-facing terms. Internal costs stay out of the document. The delivery screen lets an owner or administrator review the recipient, subject, message, and exact PDF before sending; invoice delivery requires an issued record.

Delivery history retains the document snapshot, digest, recipient, and provider outcome. If a response is uncertain, automatic retries stop. A “sent” result means the provider accepted the message, not that the customer read it. This extends the product from internal billing records to a deliberate customer communication workflow.

## Contract billing and accounting close

### Bill as the work progresses

Longer projects need more than one invoice at completion. A schedule of values divides the accepted price into work items, with cumulative completion recorded against each line. Progress applications deduct prior issued amounts and retainage—the portion held back until completion—to calculate only the current amount due. The customer document preserves the schedule version, prior work, current progress, and retained balance.

The workflow prevents billing beyond the agreed scope, mixing full-job and progress invoices, and releasing retainage twice. Once all work is billed and the job is complete, a separate release draft bills the retained balance. Tax and billing details still receive review before issuance.

<div class="build-note">
  <p><strong>A simple illustrative example:</strong> On a $10,000 contract with 10% retainage, $4,000 of cumulative completed work produces a $3,600 pretax application. Finishing the remaining scope produces a $5,400 application, followed by a $1,000 retainage release after completion. The three amounts reconcile to the accepted price.</p>
</div>

Approved scope changes can also carry through partial billing. The operator submits a saved estimate revision, records customer approval with evidence, and applies that exact revision to the linked job. Earlier estimates and issued invoices remain intact. A revised schedule must reconcile to the new contract total and cannot erase previously completed work.

The newer handoff carries an approved positive change into a separate schedule line while preserving earlier completion and invoices. Approval remains saved if a later application is blocked, so the operator can retry without obtaining or recording the same approval again. Ambiguous reductions still require schedule review. Discounts and reviewed tax rates and taxable shares carry from estimating into billing; changing their basis requires review. This is explicit tax treatment, not automatic jurisdiction lookup.

### Carry deposits, credits, and refunds through billing

A customer advance is recorded once against the accepted job. Available credit applies to its oldest issued balances, including progress applications and retainage, while any unused portion remains identifiable. Deposit applications become linked invoice receipts rather than new revenue. Duplicate references are checked across deposit and invoice-payment records.

Credits retain the original invoice and reduce revenue once. Recorded refunds link to their original receipts and reduce collections. Corrections append reversals; they do not erase the original history. Customer documents show original totals, adjustments, payments, and the remaining balance separately. Cancelling work preserves incurred costs and does not assume that every completed item should be credited.

### Repeat the service without recreating the setup

Service agreements preserve instructions, skills, locations, forms, and checklists for later visits. Each visit starts with fresh completion, crew, actual-cost, and payment records while retaining its project and campaign lineage. Automatic generation can prepare visits up to one week ahead; pausing or ending an agreement controls future generation without deleting its history.

Later invoices reuse eligible billing details and reviewed pricing/tax. Deposits, adjustments, and payments flow through the same balance calculation used by reports and customer checkout.

### Carry reviewed costs into an accounting workpaper

Accounting close connects the latest cost-to-complete review with native invoices and their explicitly linked accounting identities. Differences in amounts, balances, status, or duplicate imported records block preparation until reviewed.

The work-in-progress (WIP) workpaper uses a reviewed cost-to-cost method to calculate earned revenue, under- or overbilling, and the remaining expected-loss reserve. It freezes the inputs and prepares a balanced adjustment against reviewed opening balances. A stable journal can be exported for external posting; confirming its posting advances the opening position for the next close, so unchanged figures do not create another adjustment.

Configured QuickBooks and Xero connections add a separate approval to post that immutable journal. The workflow checks the connected company, eligible account mappings, and returned journal amounts before recording success. If the provider response is lost or ambiguous, it blocks another transmission and requires recovery against a verified provider journal ID.

This is a reviewed project-accounting workflow. The external ledger remains authoritative for invoice, tax, cash, and cost postings. Accounting-policy eligibility, historical cutoffs, bank reconciliation, and certified billing forms remain outside the implementation; the workpaper does not establish universal accounting compliance.

## Accounting and payment automation

After reviewed setup, issued invoices, receipts, applied deposits, credits, and adjustments can enter a durable queue for QuickBooks or Xero. Each transmission preserves its request and verified provider identity. Historical invoices require an explicit match; customer names and equal amounts are not enough to establish one. Linked imported copies remain excluded from duplicate revenue.

Eligible payments, credits, refunds, and reversals entered in accounting can return to the native invoice history without being exported back again. Before automatic collection, the workflow checks the provider invoice and imports eligible activity: an externally paid invoice can clear its balance without another charge. Unresolved differences block collection and require reconciliation.

Recurring Stripe collection requires the customer's explicit authorization of an amount cap and cadence. Setup does not charge the card. Changed scope, paused agreements, revoked consent, missing details, and amounts above the authorization are held. Original payment identities survive retries and lost responses; declined or authentication-required payments need attention.

Provider acceptance remains simulated in the reviewed tests. The October 25 release inspection found no enabled production accounting/payment connections. Live setup and sandbox acceptance are required before enabling collection. These are billing handoffs, not bank reconciliation or a complete general ledger; recording a refund does not itself transfer money.

### Finish commercial work from the assigned job

The new field job desk brings history, related scope, approval, completion, invoicing, and payment into one connected workflow. Other technicians continue submitting reports for review. Narrower technician sales and collection permissions remain future work. Offline reporting stays available; commercial actions use the authenticated online app and financial records are not cached in the offline shell.

### Give technicians the context to finish the job

Assigned field work carries the customer, site, asset, scope, and job tasks into a limited employee view. Technicians submit actual hours, internal observations, a separate customer-facing summary, files, and revision-bound signatures. Office review remains responsible for completion and cost decisions. Service agreements can generate dated visits from reviewed scope.

Guided photos turn documentation into a sequence: work area, asset identification, before condition, individual tasks, and finished result. The in-app camera shows a framing guide and the selected prompt, then offers retake, notes, and review. Device-selected images use their selection time and do not inherit the technician’s current location as their original capture location. Device time and reported coordinates are contextual evidence, not independently verified measurements.

### Plan and reserve several visits together

Dispatchers can preview a day plan across selected jobs and employees using visit durations, travel buffers, skills, certification expiry, local availability, and approved leave. Unplaced visits retain an explanation. One reviewed command reserves the proposed visits together; a newly approved absence or conflicting visit rejects the whole plan before partial bookings can appear.

Arrival promises now persist with the job and constrain later planning. Changing or clearing a promise requires a recorded reason. Releasing a reserved slot for replanning preserves the customer's promised window.

The optional road planner evaluates up to four technicians and nine stops from a shared depot. Reviewed address coordinates, skills, leave, existing reservations, and arrival commitments constrain the proposed routes. Return-to-depot travel occupies technician time after the last job. The routing method is a bounded heuristic; live Mapbox use needs configuration, and the planner does not promise a globally optimal fleet schedule or live traffic monitoring.

### Capture the right evidence with versioned forms

Office staff can author or copy job forms with required answers, measurement units and limits, conditional questions, dates, choices, and equipment barcodes. Assigned technicians complete them alongside photos and signatures, including offline submissions. Scanner support has a manual-entry fallback.

Each report retains its form definition so later edits cannot silently change what the technician was asked to provide. Server validation checks the version, visible questions, required answers, and data types. Changed requirements lead to review and resubmission; office staff still control completion, stock consumption, costs, and billing.

### Reopen downloaded assignments offline

Technicians can explicitly download assigned jobs to a trusted device, then reopen a dedicated field application without a network connection—even after closing the page. Downloads expire after seven days and remain scoped to the user and workspace. Signing out or removing downloads clears the assignment snapshots.

An IndexedDB outbox retains reports, photos, files, and signatures. Queued evidence is visibly separate from server-confirmed evidence, and uploads show completion only after server acknowledgement. Retries reuse the same identity, recheck current assignment and access, and hold conflicts for review. Unsynced work requires reauthorization before upload; background synchronization and unlimited storage are not promised.

Submitted reports feed an office review queue that exposes missing checklist evidence and completion-cost exceptions. That handoff connects field documentation to reviewed job completion and invoice preparation.

## Engineering decisions

### Shared business rules, separate execution contexts

The application uses Next.js, React, and TypeScript, with Supabase authentication, Postgres, row-level security, and private file storage. Vercel supports deployment and scheduled work; AI Gateway and provider-specific adapters support configured live workflows.

Demo and authenticated surfaces reuse business rules while keeping state isolated. Demonstrating an estimate, scenario, or project does not require customer records or invoke a live delivery adapter. Production failures do not silently fall back to demo persistence.

### Permissions follow the workspace

A common module inventory drives navigation and server enforcement. Customer organizations have their own memberships and enabled modules; agency access is explicitly delegated. Role and tenant checks happen at the API and database layers. Hiding a button is only the interface expression of an enforced permission.

The Members & permissions panel gives direct workspace owners and administrators control over member roles, existing-account additions, and removals. Commands recheck current authority, protect the owner and the actor’s own membership, reject stale edits, count reserved seats, and write before/after roles to an audit log in the same transaction. Removing direct membership does not silently revoke a separate agency delegation.

Custom roles narrow licensed-module capabilities for viewing, editing, exporting, and approving. Own- and team-scoped access limits assigned CRM deals and whole projects without granting unrestricted reports. These restrictions are enforced on subsequent requests; the separate technician view continues to use its field-assignment boundary.

### Protect the handoffs, not just the happy path

Operational edits use revision checks and retained history. Stale previews are rejected before a recipe or schedule update is applied. Database uniqueness protects deal-to-project handoffs from duplication.

Billing uses separate normalized tables for invoices, immutable payment entries, invoice events, and idempotent commands. Atomic database operations and locks protect repeated requests and concurrent changes. The design preserves what happened, even when an operator needs to correct it.

### Make accumulated work practical to use

Nineteen Business collections now persist as tenant-keyed records with a small revision control row. Original migration snapshots and immutable ledger history remain available; cursor-paged history and explicit exports avoid a lifetime document-size ceiling. The main Operations command path still validates complete workspace state, so these changes do not establish unlimited scale.

I also measured the cost of shared interface behavior. In a local browser fixture with 25 records, closed attachment, conversation, and signature panels previously made **100 initial requests**. Deferring their contents until first opening reduced that count to **zero**, while preserving drafts after an opened panel closes. Switching records clears the old draft.

The shared table now reuses an internationalized collator and calculates each row’s sort value once. A synthetic 10,000-row benchmark dropped from **794.56 ms to 36.47 ms**, with identical ordered output. Those are local sorting measurements—not page-load times or production latency claims.

## Migration and recovery

### Bring an existing business across with reconciliation

Reviewed CSV/XLSX batches import customer-linked equipment, service history, estimate-library prices, paused agreements, original documents, and opening receivables. Stable source IDs preserve identity. Original files are checksum-verified, and source counts and outstanding balances are reconciled before commit. A held row blocks the batch; repeated imports cannot add the same records or balances again.

Historical service records do not invent new job costs. Opening receivables do not become newly earned revenue or duplicate native checkout invoices. Accounting provider IDs let later synchronization update the same balance. Agreements remain paused for cutover review. Customers must be imported first, and batches are currently limited to 200 rows; larger resumable migrations and saved mappings remain future work.

### Test recovery and competing requests

I added encrypted backup tooling for database records, roles, audit/history tables, original stored files, and application encryption keys. Verification checks the archive before an isolated restore and compares restored table fingerprints and original files. The restore tool refuses remote or populated destinations.

The October 25 drill performed an actual PostgreSQL dump and restore of **161 tables, 93 synthetic rows, and one original file**. Restored invoices remained readable and outsider access stayed denied. Separate tests used **twelve independent database connections** to confirm one active checkout, one customer request, one logical migration result, and one mailbox lease under competing requests.

CI now exercises synthetic recovery and concurrency on changes. This is evidence that the tooling and guards work on the fixture; it is not a production backup schedule or a production-wide disaster-recovery result. Off-site backups, retention, alerts, production-sized restores, and reconstruction of hosted services and provider configuration still require operational acceptance.
