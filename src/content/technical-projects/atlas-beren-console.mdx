## The business problem

A field crew needs to know what sales promised. The person preparing the invoice needs to know what the crew delivered and what it cost. I designed ABS Console so they can both work from the accepted estimate.

## Connected records and handoffs

A customer ID connects prospect research, sales opportunities, estimates, projects, field jobs, and financial records. Each module keeps its original records and links to the others by ID.

Starting an estimate from a project brings in its customer and scope. Acceptance creates a linked job. A won opportunity can reuse its existing delivery project. An imported accounting copy can link back to the invoice that created it. Matching a name or an amount alone is too uncertain to join records automatically.

Conflicts go to a person for review. When someone saves a change, the new relationships and the records they depend on are saved together. A revision check catches cases where another person has already edited the same record.

## Revenue decisions with evidence

Sales teams can assign follow-ups, configure deal stages, and build reports from custom fields and formulas. From a saved dashboard, a manager can open the individual deals behind a forecast total.

Submitting a forecast freezes that version. Later, a manager can compare the original submission with actual wins, quotas, and commitments. Scheduled reports save the filters, dates, and underlying figures so the reader can return to what was known at the time.

The reports distinguish opportunity value from invoiced revenue and flag missing close dates and amounts.

## Connected profit and costs

The same invoice appears in customer, project, campaign, and financial reports. It contributes revenue once. The calculation accounts separately for tax, payments, deposits, and linked accounting copies.

Time, materials, other project costs, returns, credits, and reversals retain links to their original records. An imported supplier bill and its payment need to be classified so the same cost is counted once. If the source changes, the earlier review may need to be repeated.

Reports keep currencies separate. An owner can open the records behind a margin figure and see which costs are incomplete or still need assigning.

## Workspace AI Helper

Someone using **Ask AI** might ask “Which invoices are overdue?” or “What is blocking this project?” The assistant can search the records that person has permission to read and link to the sources behind its answer. It works across all seven modules.

If a customer mentions a payment and asks to change a service date in the same note, the assistant can prepare two changes for review. It flags missing amounts, uncertain customer matches, or missing evidence of acceptance.

The person approving each change needs the same permissions as someone editing the record directly. The same validation and revision checks apply. Drawing analysis and accounting reviews open the relevant workspace. Conversation history and metered usage respect the user’s access.

## Automation that explains its actions

Before a recipe runs, an operator can see which values met its conditions, which branch it selected, and what it proposes to change. A database worker takes scheduled tasks in batches, checks permissions again, and guards against repeating a completed action.

Run history shows each attempt, failure, and completed step. An administrator can cancel eligible work with a reason or retest a failure against the latest records. If an outside service gives an uncertain answer, the system holds the action for reconciliation before anyone tries again.

## Customer portal

Customers can open the saved version of a quote, approve it, request service, choose an available appointment, and see their service history and bills. Access requires a verified customer identity. Internal notes and private costs stay with the office.

Changing a quote requires fresh approval. Booking an appointment rechecks staffing and reservations before confirming the linked job.

## Business Simulator

The simulator lets an owner test a proposed job against 13 weeks of qualified labor, material availability, and daily cash. They can change the deposit, start date, or staffing and see how each option affects the plan.

Saving a prediction preserves the inputs, their timestamps, and the calculated result. The team can later compare it with recorded outcomes. Booking the job, scheduling the crew, buying stock, and posting accounting entries are separate actions.

## Migration and recovery

CSV and spreadsheet imports carry over customers, equipment, service history, price lists, agreements, documents, and opening balances. A preview identifies conflicts and held rows. An approved import commits in one transaction, and repeat submissions return the original result.

Recovery tools create encrypted bundles of database records and documents with manifests for verification. Testing included an isolated restore with synthetic records, permission checks, and concurrent requests. Production recovery still depends on the customer’s hosting, storage, identity, and provider configuration.

## Delivery and boundaries

Database policies separate each organization’s records. Module permissions and review steps govern who can change them. Unit and database tests cover calculations and access. Browser tests follow complete workflows using fictional records.

This description covers the October 28, 2026 version. The console includes adapters for email, calendars, accounting, payments, research, and automation. Enabling one for a customer requires configuration, provider authorization, and live testing. Marketing tools cover research, planning, and measurement. Publishing campaigns and managing live ads are outside this version’s scope.
